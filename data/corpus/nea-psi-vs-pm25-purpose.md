# Which index governs which decision: 24-hour PSI versus 1-hour PM2.5

NEA publishes two readings that are frequently confused. They answer different
questions and are not interchangeable.

**24-hour PSI.** A 24-hour rolling average. Because it is averaged over a long
window it is less volatile, and NEA states that individuals, institutions and
companies will find it suitable for *forward planning* — for example, deciding
the next day's outdoor activities or events that run over many hours.

**1-hour PM2.5 concentration.** The average PM2.5 concentration over the past
hour, in micrograms per cubic metre. NEA states these readings are meant for
*immediate decisions*, such as whether to go for a walk or run outdoors now.

The operational consequence: a decision taken this evening about tomorrow
morning's excursion is a 24-hour PSI decision taken against a forecast. A
decision taken at 08:55 about whether the 09:00 outdoor session goes ahead is a
1-hour PM2.5 decision taken against the current reading.

Applying a 1-hour PM2.5 value where the 24-hour PSI governs, or the reverse, is
a category error even when both readings are correctly reported.
